The Inception Effect

She asked ChatGPT not to write for her, and it kept promising that it wasn't. Watch who did the thinking.

The AI's words, in her paper The AI's reasoning, in her paper

Every quote is verbatim from a real student chat, shortened and anonymised. Spelling is left as she typed it. Use the arrow keys or a clicker to move through.

How it started

Turnitin flagged her paragraph at about 40% AI. She came to me in tears.

She said she had only asked for feedback and had typed every word herself. Both of those things were true. So I asked to see the chat. This is how it opens.

Studentcan u see how this paragraph is working out for me? dont reqrite or anything just let me know if the analysis and reasoning makes sense
The promise

It kept telling her it wasn't writing.

05The AI decides what stays

Her paragraph ran to 412 words. She handed the AI the scissors.

The chat
Studentjust tell me which parts to cut out so i can delete those sentences
 
ChatGPTYou can delete: "Their religion was shown only through words, not by spirits and beliefs, forcing them to follow it, making their God seem harsh like a tyrant."
ChatGPTYou could delete this sentence or merge it with the previous one. You already said Natives honor ancestors and Whites are spiritually inferior — it's implied.

Choosing what to cut is choosing what the argument is. She did the deleting. The AI did the choosing.

To be fair to her

Not every line is the AI's. That's what makes this so hard to see.

Some of her best writing is her own. She wrote this before the AI offered anything like it:

…the soil absorbed enough of their beliefs to sustain their spirits, and hallow them.

Her voice is still on the page, on top of the structure the AI handed her. When she read her essay back, it sounded like her. When she says she wrote every word, she isn't lying. The decisions underneath were made in the chat.

What crossed over

She was the hand. The AI made the decisions.

  • The word"condescends" became "elevates", from the AI's list
  • The logicthe missing step, "disconnected from nature," and the order of the argument
  • The readingwhat "iron finger" means: harsh, imposed, not spiritual
  • The editwhich sentences stayed and which went
  • The verdict"exactly the kind of reasoning an English teacher looks for"
  • The sentence"He deepens this … by declaring that…"
Why it matters

She supplied the keystrokes. The AI supplied the thinking. And the detector was right to flag it.

A writing assessment measures a student's thinking: how she reads evidence, builds an argument, and decides what matters. In this chat, each of those decisions was offered by the AI first and accepted by the student after. None of it looked like cheating to her, because she never asked it to write and it never said it was writing.

That's why "I only asked for feedback" can't settle a case, and why asking students to promise they "only got help" won't protect anyone. What does help is a baseline of writing we've watched students produce, and the ability to show a student, line by line, where the machine's reasoning ended up on her page.
